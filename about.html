Hi,I’m Amanda.

I’m a wife, a mom, a technology leader, and a hobbyist of hobbies. Depending on the day, the order of those things probably changes.

I have ADHD and the kind of curiosity that can turn “huh, that’s interesting” into a full-blown research project surprisingly quickly.

Some interests have been part of my life for decades. Others come and go, or disappear for a few years before I suddenly remember how much I loved them. I’ve accumulated a lot of hobbies this way, along with the supplies that go with them.

At some point I stopped feeling like I needed to pick a thing and stick with it. Turns out, being interested in lots of things is kind of my thing.

The rabbit holes are kind of the point

Plants have been one of the longer-running ones.

I started collecting succulents because I liked them. Then I wanted to know what they were called. Knowing the names led to taxonomy, which led to documenting my collection and digging through old photos to figure out what I used to have.

Now I have an extensive collection of plants with accession numbers.

That escalated.

Other interests have gone in completely different directions. I’ve picked up hobbies because something looked fun, because I wanted to make a specific thing, because someone I love was interested in it, or because I encountered something and needed to understand how it worked.

Somewhere in my house there is almost certainly a box of supplies from one of them waiting patiently for me to circle back.

I tend to want to understand things

Some of the deepest dives I’ve taken have come from trying to understand my own life and the people I love.

I’ve been married, divorced, and married again. I’ve had to learn about narcissistic abuse and codependency because those words eventually gave me language for things I had lived through but didn’t fully understand at the time.

Parenting has sent me searching for answers more times than I could count. Learning that I have ADHD changed the way I understood parts of myself I’d spent years trying to explain.

When something matters to me, I want to understand it. Sometimes that means learning the taxonomy of a plant or figuring out why a 3D print keeps failing.

Sometimes the thing I’m trying to understand is myself.

A little corner of the internet

Before MySpace and Facebook, I had a GeoCities website.

I learned HTML as a kid and built pages in Notepad and FrontPage. There was definitely a Shakira fan page in there somewhere.

Later, I had my own website for years, mostly for my photography. Social media eventually became where I shared things, and somewhere along the way I stopped maintaining a personal site.

I’ve missed having one.

There’s something I like about having my own place for all of this. It doesn’t have to fit the format of whatever platform I happen to be using, and it can change as I do.

So I made myself another little corner of the internet.

That’s just amanda.

And lately, I build things

Technology has been part of my life since I was a kid, and it has been my career for my entire adult life.

AI has changed what I’m able to do with it.

There are a lot of things I’ve thought about over the years and dismissed because I didn’t have the particular development skills required to build them. I could understand the technology, figure out what I wanted something to do, and usually come up with ideas for how it might work. Actually turning that into working software was a different story.

That gap has gotten a whole lot smaller.

AI doesn’t magically make me know how to do everything. I still spend plenty of time figuring out why something broke, changing my mind about how it should work, discovering limitations I didn’t know existed, and realizing that the supposedly simple feature I want is absolutely not simple.

But now I can get much further.

Recently, that has meant building apps for problems that exist mostly because of my own hobbies.

I did not see that becoming a hobby of its own, but here we are.

A living archive

I think of this site as a living archive. It’s somewhere I can keep all these things, but it’s also somewhere I can send people when they ask me about them.

Want a book recommendation? I have opinions.

Want to learn to crochet? I can show you what helped me get started, which tools I actually use, and which ones you probably don’t need.

I inherited a 3D printer because my son was interested in 3D printing, which meant I needed to learn how to use it. There were slicers and filament and calibration and a truly impressive number of failed prints. I know considerably more about 3D printing now than I ever expected to, including a bunch of things I wish I’d known when we started.

Plants have their own stories here. So do chickens, photographs, things I’ve made, and projects I’ve decided were a good idea.

There’s room for the less tangible things I’ve learned along the way, too.
