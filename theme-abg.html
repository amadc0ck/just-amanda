What I found doing it

The illustrations are the real obstacle, not the CSS.

Every piece of artwork on the site, the hero, the five section cards, the sprigs, the dividers, is painted in sage, lavender and the old terracotta. Toggle back and forth and watch the art: the interface changes and the pictures do not. ABG's agave blue and cactus pink appear nowhere in them. A palette swap is a day; repainting sixty-odd illustrations to match is the actual project.

There is no purple in ABG, and purple is carrying meaning.

Amethyst means “something to read” across the whole site, the journal strip, post cards, the Books card, even a button variant. ABG offers two candidates. I used Agave Blue rather than Cactus Flower Pink, because pink sits beside Terra Cotta on the wheel and the reading/action distinction would go muddy exactly where it matters most. Blue against terracotta stays unmistakable. It does make the site cooler and less romantic.

The accents are not weak, they were built for the dark ground.

On Warm Cream, Terra Cotta manages 2.74:1, Agave Blue 2.32:1 and Aloe Bloom Orange 1.77:1, all failing, all worse than the site has now. On ABG's own #243E36 ground the same three read 3.69, 4.35 and 5.71. Nothing was wrong with the palette; the light preview was simply testing it against a background it was never drawn for. Only Aloe Green inverts, fine on cream at 3.45, failing on the dark at 2.92.

One ink is not enough, and that is the real cost.

justamanda.net has a single --ink because it has a single ground. The app has --on-ground and --on-panel because it has two: cream type on the green field, charcoal type on the cream panels. Adopting the dark scheme is therefore not a token swap at all, it means splitting the ink token in two and auditing every place the stylesheet says var(--ink). The same split hits the primary button, which inverts to cream on the dark field and must invert back inside a panel.

The illustrations survive the dark ground, the logo does not.

Every image on the site carries real transparency (a tRNS chunk, checked, all of them), so the watercolours sit on the green field without a white box around them, and they look better there than on cream. But the wordmark and the round badge are Deep Garden Green artwork: on a Deep Garden Green ground they vanish. Rather than recut both in cream, the masthead and footer here stay on the panel colour, which costs nothing, keeps the art as drawn, and gives the page a structure of its own: light chrome, dark field, light chrome.

The swap is about 80% token, 20% hand work.

Most of the stylesheet reads var(--terracotta) and follows a token change for free. But 31 places across 25 distinct literals hardcode a brand colour as an rgba(), every hover tint, the card top bar, the tags, the hero band, the section hairline. A token swap alone leaves all of them the old colour. The override block at the top of this file is that work, done; it is the honest price of the migration.

Worth saying out loud: they are two brands.

The stylesheet's own opening comment says the garden app's palette is “deliberately NOT” this one, a dark tool for working in versus a light space for reading. ABG is the collection's identity; justamanda.net is yours, and books, chickens and crochet live here too. Adopting it makes the whole site read as an annex of the garden.
