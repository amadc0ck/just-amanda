Books,and worlds worth moving into.

I want a world with rules, a magic system with limits, and a series long enough to settle into. Give me lore, slow-burn tension, dangerous men with questionable judgement, and characters I will still be arguing about a year later. Getting deeply invested is the point... and yes, I will stay up too late for it.

My reader DNA

Apparently I’ve wanted the same ingredients since I was twelve.

Big immersive worlds

The bigger, the better.

Interesting magic systems

Give me rules, limits, and lore.

High stakes

Danger makes the story.

Yearning + tension

Slow burn, every time.

Morally gray characters

Perfect people are boring.

Competent heroines

She has goals. She gets things done.

Long series

I want to live in these worlds for a while.

The books that built my reader brain

Roughly thirty years, roughly in order.

  1. elementary &
    high school

    EverworldK.A. Applegate Daughters of the MoonLynne Ewing Harry PotterJ.K. Rowling UgliesScott Westerfeld The Twilight SagaStephenie Meyer Caster ChroniclesGarcia & Stohl

    The series that stuck. I was the kid who finished a book at 2am and started the next one before turning the light off.

  2. 2009 – 2019

    The Hunger GamesSuzanne Collins Fifty ShadesE.L. James Not SorryElla Miles Knocked UpStacey Lynn That ManNelle L’Amour Resist MeLucia Jordan Dirty BetMelinda Minx The Room

    YA on one side and adult romance on the other, with a full Harry Potter reread somewhere in the middle. Fifty Shades gets all the attention, but the indie romance and erotica I found afterwards is where I worked out what I liked.

  3. 2019 – 2023

    UntamedGlennon Doyle How to Be an AntiracistIbram X. Kendi SwitchChip & Dan Heath Measure What MattersJohn Doerr Shadow and BoneLeigh Bardugo The Mortal InstrumentsCassandra Clare

    Two threads at once. A long stretch of nonfiction about work, change and being a person... and then in 2021 I went back to fantasy with Shadow and Bone and The Mortal Instruments.

  4. 2023 – now

    A Court of Thorns and RosesSarah J. Maas

    Romantasy, and I have not looked up since.

    ACOTAR is what brought me back to fantasy, and I still think Sarah J. Maas is wildly overrated as a writer. Both things can be true.
