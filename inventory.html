Where to check

Every entry above links to two of these: Name goes to Kew for the botany, Growing to San Marcos for the horticulture. They answer different questions and it is worth keeping them apart.

Plants of the World Online
Kew. The accepted name and its synonyms — the closest thing to a source of truth for species. Kew themselves call it advisory. It does not cover cultivars, so a good part of this list is out of its scope.
IPNI
Who published a name, where and when. An index of names, not a ruling on which are accepted — a different question from the one above.
RHS
Cultivar names in cultivation. Most succulent genera have no registration authority at all, so for a plant like ‘Fred Ives’ the trade name simply is the name.
San Marcos Growers
Care detail, hardiness, and what a plant is actually called in California horticulture. A nursery, not a taxonomic authority — which is exactly why it is useful.
Mountain Crest Gardens
Succulents specifically. Good photographs and plain care notes, which is often what you actually want.
Flora Grubb Gardens
Rich plant pages, and where more than one plant in this collection came from.
Ruth Bancroft Garden
A real dry-garden collection in the East Bay. The place to see what a mature specimen actually becomes, which no database will tell you.

The Plant List is retired. It has been static since 2013 and is still cited everywhere. A name “confirmed” there is a thirteen-year-old snapshot.
