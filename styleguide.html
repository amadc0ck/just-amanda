Colour

The six brand colours are fixed by the brand board and are not to be eyeballed again. Everything below them is derived: grounds, ink weights and the hairline. They exist so that no component ever needs a raw hex.

What the colours mean

This is the part a palette swatch cannot tell you, and the part that keeps six colours from turning into confetti. Each colour has one job. Before reaching for one, check the job matches.

Deep Forest

Text, and the primary button. The default ink for anything that is simply words.

Warm Terracotta

A place to go. Links, arrows, the buttons, section cards. Terracotta means action: if it is not clickable, it is not terracotta.

Muted Amethyst

Something to read. The journal, post cards, and the Books card on the home page. Nothing else earns it.

Soft Lavender

Ornament, never content. Section hairlines, the dash above a journal card, the band under the hero headline.

Soft Sage

The Succulents card, the sage tag, and (deepened to --sage-deep) the footer ground. Note that gold does not survive that move: #C0A15A on #4C5B3E is 2.95:1. Only cream and lavender read down there.

Gold

Divider artwork and the thinnest rules. A metallic, not a text colour.

Amethyst starts at the journal, not before it. The Books card briefly carried amethyst on the “something to read” argument. In a row of five headings it read as one card being broken rather than one card being special, so the explore row is one colour throughout and amethyst begins where the whole band supports it.

Type

Three faces, three jobs. Playfair Display for anything that announces itself, Inter for anything you actually read, Caveat for asides in her own hand. Mixing the jobs is how the page stops sounding like one person.

.hero h1 Playfair Display 500 · 60px · the em breaks its own line

A creative life,thoughtfully collected.

The lavender band is a background-image, not a pseudo-element. .hero paints a background but opens no stacking context, so a z-index:-1 layer would sit behind the section and never be seen. The em stays display:inline with box-decoration-break:clone so each line of a wrapping headline gets its own band. As a block it drew one bar the width of the longest line and trailed off into empty space.

Every card that is not a journal card carries a 2.5px terracotta bar along its top edge. It is an inset box-shadow, not a border, so the 1px outline and the corner radius are left alone, and it deepens to full terracotta on hover. It is what carries the accent colour down into the middle band, and it rhymes with the lavender dash on the journal cards.

Headings sit in Deep Forest, not terracotta. Four large serif headings in a row under a hero that already carries a terracotta line put far too much of one warm colour on the page. Terracotta stays on the arrows.

Colour on the page

Three components added together, after the home page read as washed out. The diagnosis was not a shortage of colour: it was white cards on an ivory ground: two shades of the same non-colour, so nothing had an edge. Plus four brand colours that appeared almost nowhere in the stylesheet.

The lavender band is not decoration for its own sake: the journal is the one place the site already means purple, so the ground carries the reading colour underneath the reading content.

.social footer · inline SVG, no icon font

Framed in a ring rather than set bare: at 18px an unframed mark reads as debris beside the text links. currentColor on the paths is what lets one hover rule move the icon, the ring and the wash together. The footer keeps its own smaller, paler copy of this rule, footer .social a, because the ground down there is dark; scoping rather than folding it in is what keeps this specimen visible on cream.

padding:0 is load-bearing. This row is a <nav>, so it inherited nav a{padding-bottom:4px} from the masthead and sat every icon 2px above the centre of its own circle, 8px above, 12px below. It reads as “the circles are off” long before anyone thinks to blame the main navigation.
